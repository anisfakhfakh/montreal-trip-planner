How results are recorded

  1. An automated script runs every route above through both the Trip Planner and a live Google Transit search, then saves the results to a file.
  2. Import script results loads that file, filling in the Trip Planner and Google Transit columns automatically — time, transfers, modes used, and a leg-by-leg breakdown for each (toggle between "Trip Planner" and "Google Transit" under "Leg by leg" once a route is expanded).
  3. For routes where BIXI is allowed, a Google Maps biking-directions link is checked by hand, and the resulting time and distance are entered into the Google BIXI column.
  4. Once the Trip Planner and Google numbers are filled in, a status is suggested automatically: same modes and a similar time → Match; same modes but a bigger time gap → Potential time mismatch; different modes but the Trip Planner is faster → Faster Route; different modes and not faster → Investigate. This can always be corrected by hand, with a note added to explain why.

Notes on expected differences

  • Bike-share availability: the app checks live BIXI dock data, but Google Maps' bike-share information can lag or be missing for some routes — bike-share legs won't always match exactly, and Google may not offer a biking option at all in some cases.
  • Bike legs are capped at 45 minutes of continuous riding before the app suggests swapping bikes at a dock — Google Maps has no such limit.
  • Short transfers between metro and bus (under about 20m of walking) are shown as a waiting time rather than a separate walking leg — Google Maps may still show a short walk for the same transfer.
  • Walking directions come from a different mapping source than Google's, so they can follow a slightly different — but similar-length — street path.
  • Weather conditions and the risk score are informational only and never change which route is suggested.
  • Between roughly 1:00–5:00 AM, the metro and REM don't run — routes #36–37 are deliberately scheduled in that window to confirm the app falls back to a night bus, walking, or BIXI instead of suggesting a metro or REM leg that isn't actually running.

About this tab

Each route can restrict which travel modes it's allowed to use. Routes #1–3 are simple baseline checks that always allow every mode with no walking-time limit. From route #4 onward, buses, metro, and REM stay available on every route, but BIXI bike-share is turned off for some routes, and others are limited to buses only or metro/REM only. Most of these routes also carry a maximum walking time per leg (5, 10, 15, or 20 minutes, or no limit at all).

This table shows what each route is allowed to use — not what it actually picked. The app still chooses freely among the allowed options, so a route that allows every mode can still legitimately come back walk-only or bike-only if that's genuinely the fastest way to get there. Check the "Modes used" chips on the Route list tab to see the actual outcome.
